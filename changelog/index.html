oav-p5 0.6 · framework 0.1

Written in Markdown in docs/changelog.md; this page is generated from it. GitHub ↗

What changed, newest first: what you can do now, and where the handbook explains it. oav-p5, the one line for p5.js sketches (p5.js in one line), has its own version: 🎹 → 🩺 shows it on any sketch that loads it. The framework and the site are listed by day. Every entry links its commit; the whole history is the commit log.

oav-p5#

0.6.0#

2026-10-15 · 5e75007

  • The drums keep time when the picture is heavy. The drum machine is scheduled ahead on the audio clock: with a draw() that keeps the page busy 30 ms a frame, the kick-to-kick interval varies by 0.000 ms (0.5.0: 15 ms). In a background tab the drums keep playing and the bars keep counting. ?oav_drums=frame brings back the 0.5 drum machine. Drum machine
  • One line of rhythm: startDrums("bd*4, [~ sd]*2, hh*8"). The notation comes from TidalCycles and Strudel; OAV implements it independently, from the public documentation (@openav/pattern). A typo gets a card that says which word and why. One line of rhythm
  • The drum card names its tracks in the browser's language, shows the bar number, and lights the word of the line that is playing.
  • drumPhase and beatPhase: how far into the bar and into the beat, 0 to 1, continuous. rotate(drumPhase * TWO_PI) turns once a bar.
  • Ready-made sketch: p5/examples/rhythm/.

0.5.0#

2026-10-15 · 02aa19c

  • Record a video with its sound: startRecording(), stopRecording(), recordingSaved(info). ⏺ and 📷 in the 🎹 panel need no code. Recording
  • The canvas and every sound the line makes (startSound(), the drums, an open microphone, p5.sound 1.x) go into one file: MP4 where the browser can record it, WebM elsewhere. On a phone, the card's Share button saves it to the photo library.
  • Every take is checked: no sound, WebM, an all-black video, a nearly empty file. The card says what happened and why.
  • 📷 saves the canvas as a PNG.
  • Ready-made sketch: p5/examples/record/.

0.4.0#

2026-10-15 · e629115

  • Sound: startSound('piano'). Every note from a keyboard, the 🎹 on screen or a phone is heard; 🔊 in the panel switches instruments. Sound
  • A drum machine: startDrums(), a 16-step grid with four tracks and five patterns. Each hit calls the microphone's own kickDetected(), snareDetected() and hatDetected(), so a microphone sketch follows the drums unchanged. Drum machine
  • A timeline of bars: cue(5, 'bloom') → cueFired(), barStarted(), stepStarted(). Timeline
  • What went wrong, said on the page: a card beside the 🎹, in the browser's language: what happened, why, what to press. When something goes wrong
  • 🩺 Check-up: 🩺 in the panel (or OAVp5.check()) puts the version, the microphone, MIDI, sound, drums and the wiring on one card to screenshot.
  • micLevel follows how loud it sounds, with the room's own noise as 0; micSensitivity() sets how sensitive. A knob the sketch reads itself is no longer also wired to an OPC slider: one knob, one job.
  • Ready-made sketches: p5/examples/keys/, p5/examples/drums/.

0.3.0#

2026-10-15 · d874044

  • The microphone, in the same line: micLevel, bass / mid / treble, beatDetected(). No p5.sound, p5 1.x and 2.x. Nothing asks for the microphone until the sketch reads one of these names. The microphone
  • In Hydra too: load the same file with loadScript(), then osc(10, 0.1, () => knob1 * 2).out(). In Hydra
  • Ready-made sketch: p5/examples/mic/.

0.2.4#

2026-10-14 · d2572c3 · tag p5-v0.2.4

The first tagged version; it gathers 0.1 to 0.2.4 (from 2026-10-13).

  • Read a controller the way p5 reads the mouse: knob1 … knob8, fader1 …, pad1 …, notePressed(). A MIDI controller, the controller on screen (🎹) and a phone all come in as the same MIDI. Read it like the mouse
  • OpenProcessing: OP Configurator variables follow the knobs, in the order they are declared. With OP Configurator
  • The handbook chapter, in English and Chinese. Tested on openprocessing.org with p5 2.3.4.
  • Ready-made sketches: p5/examples/particles/, p5/examples/opc/.

Framework and site#

2026-10-15#

  • MIDI controller library: every controller has its own page with its full MIDI map, in English and Chinese. New: Novation Launchkey 25 and 37 MK4, Launchpad Pro MK3, DJ TechTools Midi Fighter Twister, AKAI APC40 mkII, and the teenage engineering OP-1 field (contributed by Miffy Chen, pull request #2). How to choose a controller, LED feedback, a request form. Controllers
  • Examples: fifteen examples in five routes, each with a guide card. New: 14 timeline and 15 p5.js in one line; 02 chord garden now grows The Last Input's L-system branches. Examples
  • New pages: /p5/ and /check/, what this device supports. The handbook says what each browser can do, and the homepage says so when a device has no Web MIDI.
  • Mapping and timeline: mappings export, import and load from ?map=. The timeline package has curves, loops, cues that fire and recording.
  • Phones: the live examples take a phone remote over the public relay, with a QR code; in portrait the stage stays on top.
  • New packages: @openav/pattern (one line of rhythm), @openav/fab (can it be 3D-printed: a check, and STL, OBJ, GLB, STEP files), @openav/out (page parameters to WLED lights, serial motor boards and OSC, with a watchdog). Packages
  • For agents and search engines: llms.txt in English and Chinese, one sitemap, and MCP tools for controller profiles: list_controllers, read_profile, validate_profile, scaffold_profile.
  • A new README, and tools/release.sh: every change ships in one fixed order.
  • The site catches up with oav-p5: this page (the changelog), "oav-p5 version · framework version" on the home page and the handbook headers, a fourth door on the home page ("I use p5.js / OpenProcessing"), the ready-made sketches listed on /p5/, and example paths in the handbook that you can click. The entry script (/p5/oav-p5.js) reaches every sketch about a minute after a release.
